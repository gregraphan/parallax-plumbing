What we do

PEX, copper, and an end to the leaks

When a house starts springing leaks in different spots, the problem is usually the pipe itself, not bad luck. We replace the whole supply system in PEX or copper, route it cleanly, and coordinate the drywall patching so your home goes back together right.

Around here that often means the gray polybutylene pipe used from the late 1970s into the 90s, or the galvanized lines in older Holiday and Spring Hill homes. Both fail from the inside, and once they start, a repipe ends the cycle for good.

Signs it is time to repipe

Leaks in more than one spot

Once you have patched two or three pinhole leaks, you are usually paying to delay a repipe rather than avoid one.

Gray poly or galvanized pipe

Polybutylene and galvanized supply lines fail from the inside. If your 70s-to-90s home still has them, plan ahead.

Low pressure or rusty water

Falling water pressure and rust-tinted water are classic signs the pipe walls are corroding and closing up.

Good to know

Repiping questions, answered

How long does a whole-house repipe take? +
Most homes are a one to two day job. We work in sections so you keep water overnight, and we walk you through the plan before we start.
PEX or copper, which is better? +
Both are excellent. PEX is flexible, freeze-tolerant, and usually more affordable. Copper is time-tested and preferred by some homeowners. We explain the trade-offs for your house and quote either one.
Will you tear up my walls? +
We open only the small access points we need, and we keep them as tidy as possible. We coordinate the drywall patching so you are not left with a mess to sort out.
Do I really need a full repipe, or just a repair? +
If only one line has failed, a repair may be all you need. If the pipe material itself is failing, spot repairs just move the next leak down the line. We give you an honest read either way.
